# Pricing & Go-To-Market

Pricing is the highest-leverage decision most founders underthink, and GTM (how you actually reach buyers) is where good products die quietly. Use current competitor prices (`research.md`) as context, but price to *value*, not to match.

## Pricing models

- **Cost-plus** - cost x markup. Simple, but leaves money on the table and ignores value. Rarely optimal.
- **Competitive** - anchored to rivals. Easy, but a race to the bottom if you have no differentiation.
- **Value-based** - price as a fraction of the value/ROI you create for the customer. Usually the right answer. If you save a business $50k/yr, charging $10k is easy to justify.
- **Freemium** - free tier to acquire, paid tier to monetize. Works only if the free tier drives real conversion and the marginal cost of free users is near zero. Easy to get wrong (giving away too much).
- **Subscription / usage / tiered / seat-based / marketplace take-rate** - the *metric* you charge on matters as much as the number.

## Choose the price metric deliberately

What you charge *per* shapes behavior and perceived fairness: per seat, per usage/transaction, per outcome, flat. Good metrics scale with the value the customer gets (so their bill grows as they succeed) and are easy to predict. A mismatch (e.g. per-seat for a product used by one admin) caps your revenue.

## Estimating willingness to pay (WTP)

Don't guess. Triangulate:

- **Customer interviews** - "what do you pay today for [the alternative / the pain]?" Existing spend is the strongest anchor.
- **Value calculation** - quantify the ROI (time saved x wage, revenue gained, cost avoided) and price as a share of it.
- **Competitor anchoring** - where rivals sit tells you the range the market accepts.
- **Tests** - a pricing page with real "buy" buttons, a pre-sale, or simple A/B on price. Intent-to-pay beats stated preference every time.
- (Van Westendorp / price-sensitivity surveys exist but are weaker than real purchase signals - mention only if the user wants survey-based methods.)

Common mistake: pricing too *low*. Low price signals low value, attracts the worst customers, and starves you of margin to fund acquisition. When unsure, test higher.

## Go-to-market: match the motion to the price

GTM must fit the economics (this echoes the B2B motion table - see `b2b.md`):

- **Product-led / self-serve** - necessary at low price points; the product sells itself, growth via signups and word of mouth.
- **Sales-led** - for higher ACV; demos, pilots, human relationships; only works if the deal size covers the cost of selling.
- **Community / content / organic** - slow-compounding, low-cost; strong for founders who can build an audience or content engine instead of buying ads.
- **Channel / partnership** - someone else's distribution; powerful if you can secure it.

## First 100 customers

The general "do marketing" answer is useless. Force specificity: *exactly* where do the first 100 customers come from? Name the subreddit, the 50 companies to email, the 5 influencers, the SEO terms, the partner. A plan that can't name the first 100 is not a plan.
