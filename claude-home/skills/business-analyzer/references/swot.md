# SWOT Analysis - done properly

Most SWOTs are useless: four boxes of vague nouns nobody acts on. A good SWOT is a *prioritization and strategy* tool. The point is not to list things - it's to (1) separate internal from external honestly, (2) rank ruthlessly, and (3) cross the quadrants to generate actual strategy.

## The two distinctions people get wrong

1. **Internal vs. external.** Strengths and Weaknesses are *internal* - things you control (team, product, capital, brand, cost structure). Opportunities and Threats are *external* - things you don't (market trends, competitors, regulation, technology shifts). "We have a small team" is a weakness (internal). "The market is growing 30%/yr" is an opportunity (external). If you find yourself putting a competitor's action in Weaknesses, stop - that's a Threat.

2. **Helpful vs. harmful.** Strengths/Opportunities help; Weaknesses/Threats harm.

|  | Helpful | Harmful |
|---|---|---|
| **Internal** | Strengths | Weaknesses |
| **External** | Opportunities | Threats |

## How to build it

For each quadrant, generate candidates, then **force-rank and keep the top 2-4**. A SWOT with 30 bullets has no point of view. Be specific and evidence-based: not "good marketing" but "12% organic conversion rate, 3x the category average."

- **Strengths** - What do you do measurably better than alternatives? What's hard for a competitor to copy? Unfair advantages (proprietary data, distribution, founder expertise, cost structure)?
- **Weaknesses** - Where are you genuinely behind? Be brutal. What would a competitor attack first? What do customers complain about? Don't disguise weaknesses as strengths.
- **Opportunities** - External trends you could ride: a growing segment, a competitor stumbling, a new channel, a regulatory tailwind, an underserved niche. Must be external and not yet captured.
- **Threats** - External forces that could hurt you: new entrants, substitutes, platform/algorithm dependence, regulation, margin compression, a shift in customer behavior.

## The part everyone skips: the TOWS cross

A list of four boxes isn't strategy. The value comes from **crossing quadrants** to produce concrete moves:

- **S-O (leverage):** Which strength lets you seize which opportunity? -> invest here.
- **S-T (defend):** Which strength neutralizes which threat? -> your moat.
- **W-O (improve):** Which weakness is stopping you from capturing an opportunity? -> fix this to unlock growth.
- **W-T (survive / avoid):** Which weakness + threat combination could be fatal? -> your existential risk; mitigate or exit.

The W-T cell is where the killer risk usually lives. Always surface it.

## Output template

```
# SWOT: [Business / Idea]
*Assumptions: [anything inferred or estimated]*

## Strengths (internal, helpful)
1. [Specific, evidenced] - why it matters
2. ...

## Weaknesses (internal, harmful)
1. [Specific, honest] - why it matters
2. ...

## Opportunities (external, helpful)
1. ...

## Threats (external, harmful)
1. ...

## Strategic implications (TOWS)
- **Biggest lever (S-O):** ...
- **Your moat (S-T):** ...
- **Unlock (W-O):** ...
- **Existential risk (W-T):** ...

## Verdict & next 2 weeks
[One-paragraph point of view + 3 cheap actions to de-risk the biggest unknown]
```

Never end a SWOT at the four boxes. The implications and verdict are the whole reason to do it.
