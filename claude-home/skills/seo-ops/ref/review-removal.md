# Review removal and replies

Loaded when a negative or suspicious Google review lands: MONTHLY-OPS-GUIDE sec. 4 step 3 (reply to every
review) and sec. 5 step 4 (respond). Checks the review against Google's review policies, drafts a removal
request only if one clearly applies, and always drafts a public reply plus a no-record fallback.

Negative is not the same as removable. Most reviews violate nothing. Never claim Google will remove one.

## Input
The exact review text and the reviewer name as shown on Google. A paraphrase or screenshot alone is not
enough: ask for the text. Optional context: business type, whether the customer is on record, history.
Several reviews: one at a time. Positive review: just a short genuine thank-you. Other platforms (Yelp,
Facebook, Trustpilot): the analysis carries over, the removal request is Google-only.

## The 11 policy checks
Grounds from Google's prohibited and restricted content policy for Maps that most often fit a negative
review. Google's heading in brackets, so the right reason is picked when reporting. If a review clearly
breaks another heading (sexually explicit, violent, dangerous or illegal), flag it under that heading.

1. Fake engagement / coordinated spam [Fake engagement; Rating manipulation]: paid, coordinated or
   bulk-posted patterns (many unrelated businesses, sudden flood, review-mill profile).
2. Impersonation [Impersonation]: posing as another person, business or authority. Merely naming a real
   person is not impersonation.
3. Conflict of interest [Rating manipulation; Misrepresentation]: current or former employee, competitor,
   vendor with a grievance, personal stake.
4. Non-customer [Fake engagement, not based on a real experience]: no record of any interaction;
   describes an appointment or purchase that never happened.
5. Misinformation [Misrepresentation]: demonstrably false facts (closed that day, price not charged,
   service not offered, staff who do not exist).
6. Off-topic [Off-topic]: not about the business's products or services.
7. Harassment, offensive, profanity [Harassment; Offensive content; Obscenity and profanity]: threats,
   attacks, unsupported allegations of wrongdoing, profanity used to offend. Harsh but respectful
   criticism is allowed. Defamation is a legal question, not a policy reason: Google has a separate legal
   removal process; get legal advice and never call a review defamatory in the policy request.
8. Hate speech [Hate speech].
9. Personal information [Personal information]: private details without consent. Public-facing
   professionals working under their own name and publicly known executives may be named.
10. Advertising or solicitation [Advertising and solicitation].
11. Repetitive or defacement [Unclear and repetitive content; Defacement and mischief].

**Reviewer name** (a supporting flag, never a violation alone): handle, brand name, initials only, random
characters, obvious pseudonym. Mention after the policy assessment.

## Procedure
1. Walk the 11 checks; flag only a clear match with evidence in the review text itself.
2. Pick the single strongest category; mention a second only if it clearly applies.
3. Confidence: HIGH (text directly evidences it), MEDIUM (strong context, no admission), LOW (one soft
   signal, or rests on context not provided). A suspicious name alone is LOW at best.
4. Removal request only if a violation is found. Public reply and no-record fallback always.

Public reply shape (both languages): thank them, acknowledge the specific issue without admitting facts
not established, one line of context if useful, invite them to call or write. Colombian clients: usted
form unless the brand voice is clearly tu.

## Rules
- Conclusions only from the review text and name. Never invent the reviewer's history or the business's
  records.
- Harsh but legitimate: say so; no removal request, just the replies.
- Never offer a refund, discount or gift in exchange for removing or changing a review. Fixing the
  problem is fine; making the fix conditional on the review is not.
- Never accuse the reviewer in public of being fake, a competitor or an ex-employee, even when reporting
  on that basis. No private customer details in replies.
- Regulated industries (medical, legal, financial, allied health): the reply must not confirm the person
  was a patient or client or disclose protected information; have compliance sign off.
- Google rarely removes: star-only ratings with no text, honest negative opinions of a real experience,
  disagreement with a service decision. Say so and go straight to the reply.
