# Review engine

Loaded for the MONTHLY-OPS-GUIDE sec. 5 Reviews add-on, and when a client asks for more reviews,
testimonials, case studies or a reviews page, or a competitor's comparison page outranks them for their
own brand name. Works a ladder in order: inventory what exists, rank the bigger asks, write the outreach,
build the reviews page, then explain what feeds AI answers.

## Policy floor (read first)
- **Ask every customer the same way, at the same natural moment, with the same public link.** Google bans
  selectively soliciting positive reviews (review gating) and treats it as fake engagement. A private
  feedback channel may exist, but never as a filter in front of the public ask.
- **No incentives.** Nothing (payment, discount, free goods, entry into a draw) in exchange for a review,
  and never discourage negative reviews.
- **No stars for self-reviews.** Review snippets are not eligible when a LocalBusiness or Organization
  hosts reviews about itself, whether written on the site or embedded from a third party. AggregateRating
  produces stars only inside `Product` on a product page (genuine product reviews). For service and local
  clients, reviews are trust content and a GBP Map-Pack signal, not a rich-result goal.
- **Never invent** a review, quote, client name or figure. A fabricated testimonial is a fake review.

## Input
Business name, site, what it sells. Then whatever exists: GBP reviews, client list, testimonials in the
inbox. Ask for the client list only at step 2.

## Procedure
1. **Inventory what exists.** If you cannot see something, say so and give the exact search to run.
   Google reviews often cannot be fetched; ask for them (or count, rating, date of the latest).
   - Google reviews: count, rating, recency, reviews in the last 90 days, and which name a specific
     service (those are the quotable ones; mark them).
   - Brand mentions with no review attached: someone wrote about or linked to the client and was never
     asked for a review. Cheapest opportunity on the list.
   - Empty review profiles: industry directory, marketplace, partner or association listings. An empty
     profile on a site that already ranks is a gap; say what it takes to fill it.
2. **Rank the bigger asks** (case study, testimonial, video, interview). Interview: top clients by revenue,
   recent wins worth naming, quiet for a year, already said something nice. Short list with a reason per
   name. This ranking only decides who gets a personal nudge first for those asks; it never decides who is
   asked for a Google review (see policy floor).
3. **Write the outreach,** four distinct asks:
   - Review ask: short, at the moment the result lands (rarely invoice time), names the specific thing
     that went well. Same public link for everyone.
   - Case study ask: says the time cost and what they get; names the number to be used and confirms they
     are comfortable with it appearing.
   - Video testimonial ask: how long, which questions, who edits, where it goes.
   - Interview ask: a conversation about their results, yielding case study, quotes and video from one
     booking.
4. **Build the reviews page** (walk through it). Purpose: the result for "<brand> reviews" should be the
   client's own page, not a competitor comparison or directory listing. Real attributed quotes, links to
   case studies, a link to leave a public review. Schema: for service/local clients, no AggregateRating
   expectation (policy floor); Review markup on own testimonials will not produce stars. Fewer than five
   reviews: the page is premature, work steps 1-3.
5. **AI answers.** Check whether the client is named for buyer queries (MONTHLY-OPS-GUIDE sec. 7; Bing
   citations via `ai-citation-share.md`). If you cannot query the engines, list 3-5 queries for the
   operator to run. Never report a guess as what an AI tool says. Steps 1-4 are inputs AI systems read;
   none of them forces a citation.

## Output
1. **What exists:** table of source, what is there, what is missing, worth fixing? Ordered by impact.
2. **The quotable ones:** verbatim (typos included), reviewer first name and date.
3. **Who to ask, in order:** name, reason, which ask, best moment.
4. **The outreach:** four templates filled with real names; [brackets] for anything guessed.
5. **The reviews page:** outline, quote placement, schema and what it will and will not produce.
6. **AI answer check:** queries checked, what was found, weakest input.
7. **Not checked:** each gap with the export or login that would answer it. Never omitted.

## Rules and edge cases
- Rank by impact, not count: one named case study on a page with impressions beats ten reviews on a
  dormant profile.
- Mostly negative reviews: stop the ladder; the work is responses (`review-removal.md`) and fixing the
  underlying problem.
- Regulated industries (health, legal, financial): testimonial rules vary by market; flag and check before
  publishing.
- New business with no clients: the ladder does not apply; complete and claim the GBP first.
- Existing reviews page: audit it (ranks for "<brand> reviews"? quotes real and attributed?).
- Reviews only on a platform that forbids republishing: check its terms before lifting quotes.
- The skill writes the ask; a human sends it.
- Write in the client's language (en, or es-CO for Colombian clients).
